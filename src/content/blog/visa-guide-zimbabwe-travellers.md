Nothing kills a trip faster than a visa problem at the airport. We've seen clients turned away for a passport with fewer than six months' validity, for the wrong visa type, and — most commonly — for assuming a visa was "probably fine".

This guide walks you through the process, step by step, so you land at your destination with the right paperwork.

## Step 1: Check whether you need a visa at all

Start with the destination's official immigration website or embassy page — not a random blog. Key questions:

- Do Zimbabwean passport holders need a visa? (For many African destinations the answer is yes, but some offer visa-free or visa-on-arrival.)
- Is it **visa on arrival**, **e-visa**, or **sticker visa in advance**? These are very different processes with very different timelines.
- Does your passport need a minimum validity? **Six months is the standard** — if you're close to that, renew first.
- Do you need blank pages? Most countries want at least two.

## Step 2: Check your passport first, always

Before you pay for anything, check your passport:

- Valid for at least **6 months** beyond your travel date
- At least **2 blank pages**
- In good physical condition (no water damage, torn pages, or unofficial stamps)

Passport renewals in Zimbabwe can take several weeks, so this is the first thing to do — not the last.

## Step 3: Pick the right visa type

Tourist visa, business visa, transit visa, and e-visa are not interchangeable. A business visa doesn't cover tourism, and a tourist visa doesn't cover work. If your trip has more than one purpose (say, a conference plus sightseeing), take the strictest one that applies and mention the full itinerary in your application.

## Step 4: Apply — with the correct documents

For most e-visa and embassy applications you'll need:

- Passport scan (all info pages, not just the photo page)
- Passport photos — follow the exact size and background rules
- Flight itinerary (don't book the ticket until the visa is approved unless you're certain)
- Hotel bookings or an invitation letter
- Proof of funds (recent bank statements)
- Yellow fever certificate for many African destinations — get vaccinated and carry the card

**The classic mistake**: uploading a blurry scan or a photo with the wrong background, then wondering why the application took three times as long. Take clean, high-contrast scans.

## Step 5: Give yourself time

Rule of thumb for embassy sticker visas: **apply 4–6 weeks before departure**. E-visas are faster — usually 3–10 working days — but "usually" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. Government portals go down, payment systems reject cards, and peak season backs everything up.

If you're applying for multiple people, add extra time. A family of four's applications rarely process at the same speed.

## Step 6: Double-check the details before you fly

- Print your approved e-visa — some airlines won't let you board without seeing it.
- Make sure names match your passport **exactly**, including middle names.
- Check whether the visa is single or multiple entry — a single-entry visa won't let you pop over to a neighbouring country and come back.
- Save a digital copy on your phone in case the printout gets lost.
