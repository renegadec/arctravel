Victoria Falls is the kind of place that rewires your sense of scale. The Zambezi — one of Africa's great rivers — drops 108 metres into a gorge so wide you can stand across from it and still feel the spray on your face. Locals call it *Mosi-oa-Tunya*, "the smoke that thunders", and after your first visit you'll understand why.

This guide covers everything a first-timer needs to know, based on what our clients ask us most.

## When to go

**April to July** is the classic sweet spot. The falls are at their most powerful (peak flood is usually late April to May), the bush is lush, and game viewing is excellent.

**August to December** brings lower water, which sounds like a downgrade — until you realise it's the only time you can walk across the lip of the falls at the Knife Edge or into the Devil's Pool on the Zambian side.

**January to March** is green season: fewer crowds, lower prices, and dramatic skies. Some viewing points close when the river is in full flood, so check conditions before you book.

## Where to stay

- **Victoria Falls town (Zimbabwe side)**: closest to the falls, best restaurants, and the most convenient base for a short visit.
- **Zambezi river lodges**: five minutes outside town, quiet, and you'll often see elephants from the deck.
- **Livingstone (Zambian side)**: cheaper entry to the falls and easy access to Devil's Pool, but you'll cross the border each time you move around.

For most first-timers, we recommend three nights on the Zimbabwe side — long enough for the falls, a game drive, and one river activity without rushing.

## What you shouldn't miss

1. **The falls themselves** — take the main path along the Zimbabwe side at least once, then again at sunset when the light turns the gorge gold.
2. **A helicopter or microlight flight over the falls** — the single best view you'll ever get of the scale of the thing. Book early; slots fill fast.
3. **Sunset cruise on the Zambezi** — hippos, crocs, and the best sundowner of your life.
4. **A game drive or walk in Zambezi National Park** — it's right next to town.
5. **Devil's Pool (seasonal)** — the famous swim at the edge of the falls. Only open at low water, so check dates.

## What to pack

- A **waterproof jacket or poncho** — you *will* get soaked near the falls. Plastic ponchos are sold at the entrance, but a proper rain jacket is better.
- **Sturdy, non-slip shoes** — the paths are wet and the rock can be slippery.
- **Insect repellent** and sunscreen.
- A dry bag for your phone and camera on the boat cruise.
- Cash in USD for tips, local vendors, and smaller restaurants — card works at bigger hotels and lodges, but cash is still king at the market.

## Practical notes

- **Visa**: many nationalities get a visa on arrival in Zimbabwe, but rules change. Check requirements before you travel — our [visa assistance service](/services/visa-assistance) handles this for you.
- **Money**: Zimbabwe uses USD as its main currency, plus bond notes and ZiG for small change. Bring clean, newer notes — older or marked bills are often refused.
- **Getting there**: fly into Victoria Falls Airport (VFA) from Harare, Johannesburg, or Nairobi, or drive from Bulawayo. If you're combining with Hwange, it's roughly a 3-hour drive between the two.
- **Time your visit**: the falls are best in the morning for light and spray visibility; the boat cruise is best around 4–5pm.
